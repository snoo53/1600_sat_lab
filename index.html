Why not just use Bluebook

College Board gives away eight full-length adaptive practice tests in Bluebook, plus a large official question bank. That material is free, it is adaptive, and it is written by the people who write the exam. Use it first, and use all of it.

If you are scoring below 1400, finish the official tests before you buy anything here. The free sample pack says exactly that, and this page will not contradict it. Most of the points available to you at that stage are still in the official material.

Buy a test from us when the official material is exhausted and the remaining gap sits at the top of the range — when you are already at 1450 or above, the questions you miss are few, and each one turns on a distractor you cannot quite explain after the fact.

This is not a substitute for official practice. It is what you use after it, and it is built to be harder in a specific, documented way rather than harder in general.

What you are actually paying for

Four claims, each of which can be checked against the files you receive.

1

Every distractor is engineered, not filler

Each wrong answer targets a documented misconception, and the answer key states which one and why a strong student would fall for it. In Test 1, 85 of 98 items carry an explicit distractor-logic block — that is every multiple-choice item. The remaining 13 are student-produced-response items, which have no answer choices by construction.

2

Independent cold-solve review

Every item is solved from scratch by a reviewer who did not write it, then audited for double-keys, answer-position patterns, cross-module repeats and lexical give-aways before release. Where the audit flagged something worth disclosing, it stays visible: 18 items in Test 1 carry a "cold-solve watch item" note in the answer key.

3

Difficulty is disclosed, not asserted

Each module publishes its item-difficulty counts in the answer key, and the same counts are republished below. Two labelling scales are in use, which is worth stating plainly: the Reading and Writing modules are graded on a Band 5/6/7 scale, while Math Module 1 was graded easy/medium/hard and was not audited on the band scale.

4

Purpose-built scoring curve

Scaled scores come from a hard-path raw-to-scaled curve built for this difficulty profile rather than a curve copied from an official test. Reading and Writing is scored over 54 questions and Math over 44; a 1500 target corresponds to roughly 50 or more of 54 raw in Reading and Writing and 40 or more of 44 raw in Math.

The first modules are deliberately lighter — they are the on-ramp, matching how the real exam's first module is not the hard path. The weight of this test sits in the second modules.

This practice test is non-adaptive. The real Digital SAT routes you to a Module 2 difficulty based on your Module 1 performance; this test does not. The scoring guide also discloses its other sources of imprecision: equal item weighting, no adaptive routing, and no empirical calibration against a representative student sample. Any scaled score you get here is a pilot estimate from a disclosed internal curve, not an official College Board score.
